# AI Assistance, Human Authorship and Creative Provenance

**Status:** Proposed creative-practice policy  
**Canonical policy:** [FELOH Corpus — AI-Assisted Development and Source Integrity](https://github.com/donfelohke/feloh-corpus/blob/main/AI-ASSISTED-DEVELOPMENT.md)

GENJE applies the canonical policy to music, sound, samples, livecoding, audiovisual work, choreography, movement research, writing, images and performance documentation.

## Rules

- Human authorship, intent, selection, performance and release responsibility must remain explicit.
- Record collaborators, performers, cultural/source references and material third-party inputs.
- Record material AI assistance when it shapes a released work, text, image, audio, code, score or research interpretation.
- Do not present generated or illustrative material as documentary evidence, a real performance, a real participant or an independently authored source.
- Never use AI transformation to disguise the origin of unauthorised source code, music, samples, images or other restricted work.
- Check the relevant licence, permission, attribution, consent and distribution limits for every included asset.
- Do not upload private rehearsal recordings, participant information, unreleased collaborator material or confidential correspondence to external AI services without explicit authority.
- Keep source versions, tool/runtime versions, dependencies and execution conditions where reproducibility is claimed.
- AI output is not a substitute for cultural research, attribution, consent or human review.

## Licensing boundary

The existing GPL-3.0 repository licence does not automatically cover third-party music, samples, recordings, photographs, artwork or participant performances. Record asset-specific rights in the manifest or work record. Do not infer permission from public availability.
