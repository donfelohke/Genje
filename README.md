GENJE is the cultural and creative field of the FELOH practice: Genge-rooted sound and aesthetics, AfroTech, livecoding, audiovisual experimentation, movement, choreography and choreohacking. Sound, code and embodied movement can meet without reducing culture or people to generic data.

“The Fire of the Formidable” is an adopted creative interpretation and identity line. It is not presented as a verified universal translation or etymological claim. Ordinary usage and etymology require contextual evidence.

## Creative design language

The design should feel authored, rhythmically alive and culturally situated: Genge energy, African sonic and material references, embodied gesture, computational pattern and considered editorial composition. Working visual vocabulary includes Ghetto-Luxe Dark Academia and Silicon Savannah neo-naturalism. These are directional references, not permission to flatten diverse African cultures into one aesthetic.

Use strong hierarchy, deliberate negative space, tactile material cues and legible typography. Avoid generic luxury templates, decorative tech clutter, forced slang, unsupported cultural claims, default Rasta shorthand, neon-AI haze and collage without compositional logic. Keep the human creator and the work central.

## Practice areas

- **Sound:** Genge, AfroTech, Genge Algo, Boomba Sounds & Riddims, rhythm studies and authored compositions.
- **Live systems:** TidalCycles, SuperCollider and Hydra are tools in the explored practice; their mention does not prove a particular setup is installed or a work released.
- **Movement:** dance, choreography, improvisation, Laban-informed study, LabanEditor and Odi+ exploration.
- **Choreohacking:** experiments connecting movement scores, timing, sound, code and performance.
- **Documentation:** preserve context, collaborators, versions, rights, consent, observations and limits.

## Maturity and evidence

Label each work accurately: LIVE, PROTOTYPE, RESEARCH, CONCEPT, HISTORICAL, SUPERSEDED or UNKNOWN. A concept, document, source file, repository or recording is not by itself proof of a released system or reproducible performance. Record what was actually tested or performed.
