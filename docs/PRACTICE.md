# GENJE — Practice Framework

This document describes practice areas and a minimum record. It does not claim that every workflow, instrument, software package or performance system is currently implemented.

## 1. Sound and rhythm

Practice includes Genge-rooted sound and aesthetics, AfroTech, Genge Algo, Boomba Sounds & Riddims, rhythm studies, composition and improvisation. Record the cultural/source context of references and distinguish original material from samples, quotations and external assets.

## 2. Livecoding and audiovisual work

TidalCycles, SuperCollider and Hydra are part of the explored tool vocabulary. For each actual work, record:
- Software/runtime and exact versions.
- Source repository and commit.
- Hardware, operating system and dependencies.
- Audio/visual routing, clock or timing assumptions.
- Samples, external media, licences and permissions.
- Setup and execution steps.
- What was tested or performed and what was observed.

A tool appearing in a design document is not evidence that it is installed, configured or used in a released work.

## 3. Movement and choreography

Practice includes dance, choreography, improvisation, embodied composition and Laban-informed movement research. Laban notation, LabanEditor and Odi+ may be explored as references or tools; identify the exact notation system, version and method used before making formal technical claims.

Document score, movement vocabulary, spatial and temporal context, adaptation, performer agency, accessibility and consent. Do not turn participant movement or bodies into generic datasets or automated judgements.

## 4. Choreohacking

Choreohacking is an exploratory practice connecting movement scores, rhythm, timing, code, sound and performance. Treat each experiment as situated research. State the question, human decisions, input materials, transformations, performer choices, observed result and limitations. Do not imply that a repeatable system exists until its implementation and tests are recorded.

## 5. Session record

Minimum fields:
- Date and context/place, if safe to publish.
- Participants and roles, with consent and privacy respected.
- Intent, score or research question.
- Tools, instruments, software and versions actually used.
- Source commit or working materials.
- What was recorded (audio/video/code/notation) and where.
- Rights, licences, credits and consent notes.
- Observations, deviations, limitations and next steps.
- Gallery asset IDs, where applicable.

## 6. Release discipline

A session note is not a published work. A recording is not proof of reproducibility. A prototype is not a released platform. Before public release, review authorship, cultural context, credits, rights, consent, accessibility, privacy and the accuracy of all claims.
