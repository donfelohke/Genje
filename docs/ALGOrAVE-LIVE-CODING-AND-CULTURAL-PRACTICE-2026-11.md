## Community reference

[Algorave — General guidelines](https://github.com/Algorave/guidelines/blob/master/README_en.md) is a community-sourced document, not a franchise rulebook, legal standard or complete history. It explicitly encourages people to host and promote events while recognising Algorave as a community.

The current English README emphasises:
- algorithmic and code-based processes should often be visible in music and visuals, while recognising that not every act must be live-coded or project its screen;
- artistic diversity and experimentation matter more than a single prescribed format;
- avoid rigid headliner hierarchies and excessive self-promotion;
- recognise that algorithmic dance music sits within a much longer history;
- build diverse, accessible and safe events and communities;
- consider affordability, codes of conduct and local community relationships;
- connect with existing live-coding networks and share resources.

These are useful principles for GENJE, but should be applied contextually rather than copied as a rigid checklist.

## GENJE application

GENJE's creative scope includes Genge-rooted sound, AfroTech, algorithmic music, live coding, audiovisual performance, movement, choreography and choreohacking. The Algorave reference strengthens one part of that practice; it does not define the whole of GENJE or displace its Kenyan and African cultural contexts.

For each performance or event, document:
1. artistic intent and relationship to the relevant musical / cultural histories;
2. whether code or algorithmic processes are used, and what is visible to the audience;
3. performers, collaborators, credits and authorship;
4. samples, software, datasets, licences and other source provenance;
5. consent, accessibility, safety and audience-care arrangements;
6. venue, technical setup, failure plan and documentation permissions;
7. audience access and affordability where the event is ticketed;
8. post-event observations without inventing audience response or impact.

## African historical context

Use [UNESCO's General History of Africa](https://www.unesco.org/en/general-history-africa) as a major scholarly gateway for historical context, and select specific volumes and bibliographies by period and region. Do not use a broad continental synthesis as a substitute for local histories, community knowledge, music scholarship or primary sources.

In particular, avoid describing live coding or Algorave as the future or culmination of African music. Treat contemporary computational practice as one situated practice in longer and diverse histories of rhythm, sound, dance, technology, exchange and cultural innovation. Any historical connection must be researched and evidenced, not asserted by analogy.

## Practice boundary

- Community guidelines are guidance, not law or certification.
- A live-coding event is not automatically inclusive or safe; operational measures matter.
- Visible code is an artistic choice and context, not a universal requirement for every GENJE work.
- Do not copy Algorave branding, logos, photographs or third-party assets without checking their rights.
- Preserve local authorship and cultural specificity; avoid flattening African cultures into one aesthetic.
