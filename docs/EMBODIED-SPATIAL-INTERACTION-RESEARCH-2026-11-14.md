# Embodied Spatial Interaction — GENJE Research Note

**Date:** 2026-11-14  
**Status:** RESEARCH  
**Scope:** GENJE's movement, choreography, livecoding and audiovisual practice in relation to IKAI MIRROR+ spatial-computing research.

## 1. Domain boundary

GENJE contributes the embodied and creative research questions: movement, rhythm, choreography, Laban-informed study, improvisation, livecoding and audiovisual expression. IKAI owns the reflective participant interface; WARENA owns compute and spatial-runtime infrastructure; FELOH Corpus owns shared governance and evidence.

GENJE does not become a sensor platform, a person-analysis product or a commercial checkout domain.

## 2. Research question

How can spatial computing support movement as an authored, contextual and expressive practice—allowing performers to explore spatial relationships, timing, gesture, rhythm and audiovisual composition—without treating the body as a biometric identity, behavioural profile or score?

## 3. Candidate interaction patterns

- Participant-directed spatial annotations and movement pathways.
- Choreographic scores and timing cues displayed in physical or virtual space.
- Performer-controlled recording and replay of selected movement sequences.
- Livecoding and audiovisual mappings explicitly chosen by the performer.
- Collaborative spatial composition where each participant controls what is shared.
- Non-camera alternatives: manual input, wearable-free interaction, fiducials on props or room surfaces, and pre-authored scores.

These are research concepts, not implemented GENJE features.

## 4. Method boundaries

- Preserve authorship, cultural context, collaborators and performance conditions.
- Distinguish movement description from interpretation and from evaluation.
- Do not infer identity, health, emotion, personality or capability from movement.
- No person scoring, ranking, biometric identification or covert recording.
- Use synthetic data, props and participant-authored notation before body tracking.
- Record consent, purpose, retention, access and deletion for any captured material.
- Treat Laban/Bartenieff frameworks as situated movement vocabularies, not universal ground truth or automatic labels.

## 5. Research questions

1. Which movement concepts are useful as authored notation rather than sensor-derived classification?
2. Can spatial overlays improve rehearsal or composition compared with paper, projection or a conventional display?
3. What timing and latency matter for live performance?
4. How should uncertainty and sensor dropout be shown rather than hidden?
5. Can performer-controlled capture and replay support learning without creating persistent profiles?
6. What can be achieved without cameras or wearables?

## 6. Relationship to IKAI MIRROR+

IKAI may provide participant-directed reflection, spatial annotations and memory. GENJE may provide creative tasks and authored movement scores. Neither should silently determine what a movement means. Interpretation remains with the participant, performer, choreographer or explicitly authorized collaborator.
