# GENJE — Orientation

**Domain:** Creative technology · Genge · AfroTech · livecoding · sound · movement · choreography · choreohacking  
**Identity:** **GENJE ZA WARENA — THE FIRE OF THE FORMIDABLE**  
**Creative principle:** **Penye NIA, Pana Njia.**  
**Human author:** Felix Lwova · Don Feloh · @donfelohke · #HisGenjeness  
**Maturity:** Creative practice / research; each work has its own evidence-based status.

## Purpose

GENJE holds the creative and embodied strand of the FELOH practice: Genge-rooted sound and aesthetics, AfroTech, algorithmic music, live systems, audiovisual experimentation, dance, movement notation, choreography, choreohacking, performance and collaborator credits.

## Meaning and boundary

“The Fire of the Formidable” is the adopted creative interpretation and campaign identity. It is not presented as a verified universal translation or etymological claim.

GENJE is a cultural and creative practice, not a separate commercial business, checkout or booking surface. Commercial beauty and digital services remain under NAKEBUSEN. WARENA remains the distinct systems-engineering identity powering NAKEBUSEN DIGITAL.

## Practice map

- **Sound and rhythm:** Genge, AfroTech, Genge Algo, Boomba Sounds & Riddims, rhythm and composition studies.
- **Livecoding and audiovisual systems:** TidalCycles, SuperCollider and Hydra are tools explored in the practice; document exact versions and actual use per work.
- **Movement and choreography:** dance, improvisation, Laban-informed movement studies, notation and choreographic composition.
- **Choreohacking:** exploratory connections between movement scores, timing, sound, code and embodied performance.
- **Cultural research:** contextual study of language, sound, forms, histories and influences with source attribution and uncertainty preserved.

## Audience

Collaborators, researchers, audiences and future practitioners of the work—not a booking cart. Commercial enquiries route through NAKEBUSEN.

## Documentation contract

For each work, record title, date, medium, human author and collaborators, cultural context, source references, tools/versions, setup and reproduction notes, rights, consent, release status, performance context, observed result and limitations. Distinguish a sketch, research experiment, rehearsal, improvisation, prototype, live performance and released work.
