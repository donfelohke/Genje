# Algorave Guidelines — Livecoding and Creative-Practice Reference

**Repository:** GENJE  
**Date:** 9 November 2026  
**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL COMMUNITY REFERENCE / RESEARCH

## Source

[Algorave — General Guidelines (English)](https://github.com/Algorave/guidelines/blob/master/README_en.md)

The document identifies itself as community-sourced guidance for participating in and organising Algorave events. It explicitly invites people to host and promote events while asking organisers to consider the wider community.

These guidelines are not a formal technical specification, a universal definition of livecoding, or an endorsement of GENJE.

## Principles worth studying

The current English guide foregrounds several practical principles:

1. **Visible algorithmic process:** many performances expose code-based or algorithmic generation of music and visuals. Live coding is one approach, not a requirement for every act.
2. **Artistic plurality:** combine code with other artforms and approaches; do not reduce the event to one software stack or performance style.
3. **Community over promotion:** be cautious about sponsorship and institutional alignment; use events to support other practitioners rather than turn the platform into self-promotion.
4. **Reduced hierarchy:** the guidelines discourage conventional headliner hierarchies and encourage a shared audience experience.
5. **Historical humility:** Algorave is not presented as the future of dance music, but as experimentation within a much longer history.
6. **Diversity and access:** consider lineups, audiences, technologies, affordability and transparent pay-what-you-can options.
7. **Safety and inclusion:** consider accessible safe spaces and a code of conduct.
8. **Local and networked community:** connect with local practitioners and use the wider livecoding community for exchange and support.

These are community recommendations. Apply them with context, care and consultation rather than turning them into rigid rules.

## GENJE interpretation

GENJE's practice connects Genge, AfroTech, livecoding, sound, audiovisual systems, movement, choreography and choreohacking. The Algorave document is useful for thinking about how algorithmic processes can become legible as performance and how a creative scene can support participation rather than status competition.

It does not define Genge, Kenyan music, Turkana culture or GENJE's identity. Do not collapse distinct histories into a generic global livecoding aesthetic. Record local context, collaborators, sources, language, musical lineage, performer intent and audience relationship.

## Practical event / performance checklist

Before an event or release, document:

- [ ] Creative intent and cultural/music context.
- [ ] Human author, performers, collaborators and role-specific credits.
- [ ] Whether algorithmic processes are visible, audible or otherwise explained—and why.
- [ ] Code repository, exact commit, runtime, dependencies and licence.
- [ ] Audio/video signal flow, instruments, devices, network and timing.
- [ ] Sample, image, video and dataset provenance and permissions.
- [ ] Performer and participant consent for recording and publication.
- [ ] Accessibility, affordability, safety and code-of-conduct arrangements.
- [ ] Community relationships, local participation and non-extractive promotion.
- [ ] Rehearsal/performance status and observed deviations from the documented setup.

The checklist is a GENJE working adaptation, not an official Algorave checklist.
