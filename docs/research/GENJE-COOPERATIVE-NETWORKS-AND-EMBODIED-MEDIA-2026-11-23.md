# GENJE — Cooperative Networks and Embodied Media

**Date:** 2026-11-23 · **Status:** RESEARCH / CREATIVE-INFRASTRUCTURE IMPLICATIONS  
**Owner:** GENJE · EXPRESSION  
**Relationship:** GENJE ZA WARENA — creative practice and technical enablement, not product ownership.

## 1. Research question

How might cooperative connectivity and self-hosted services support creator-controlled Genge-rooted sound, livecoding, audiovisual work, movement research and collaborative performance without surrendering authorship, cultural context or participant authority?

This is GENJE's bounded contribution to WARENA's infrastructure study. It does not select a platform, propose a commercial service or claim a live performance network.

## 2. Why connectivity matters

GENJE's work crosses sound, code, audiovisual experimentation, movement, choreography and choreohacking. These practices may generate or depend on:
- audio, stems, samples and session recordings;
- livecoding source, patches, software versions and performance logs;
- movement scores, notation, rehearsal notes and timing data;
- video, still images, stage plans and audiovisual compositions;
- contributor credits, licences, permissions and cultural context.

A cooperative network could support shared archives, rehearsal resources, collaborative tools and local media distribution. The goal is not simply publishing more content: it is preserving creators' ability to control, document, retrieve, license and withdraw work.

## 3. Infrastructure possibilities

### Local network and offline collaboration

A local Wi-Fi network can support rehearsal-room exchange, local media playback, collaborative source access and shared documentation without upstream internet. Mesh networking may extend connectivity between sites, but only after coverage, latency, throughput, power and maintenance requirements are measured.

### Self-hosted repositories and archives

A self-hosted source repository or media archive can preserve history, credits, licences and versioned artefacts. It also creates duties: access control, backups, restore tests, storage growth, moderation, rights review and succession. A server controlled by one person is not automatically a cooperative archive.

### Federation and creator distribution

Federated services may help independent communities exchange public posts or media references. ActivityPub is a W3C Web protocol; its use must be assessed against WARENA's current web-technology boundary. Federation is not a rights-management system and does not make redistribution lawful or consensual.

### Peer networking and privacy

GNUnet and Tor address different networking/privacy problems. Neither replaces creator permissions, encrypted backups or secure endpoints. Do not expose private rehearsal material or participant recordings merely because a network is decentralised or an onion service is available.

## 4. Authorship, rights and participant authority

Every shared artefact should retain a provenance record appropriate to its medium:
- creator/contributor names or chosen credit;
- title, date and version;
- source files and transformation history;
- software, instruments, samples and third-party materials;
- licences and permissions for included elements;
- recording/performance context;
- participant consent and capture/publication limits;
- withdrawal, correction and takedown process;
- private, group-only, public or reuse-licensed status.

Movement and performance are situated practices. Do not reduce choreography, gesture or livity to generic sensor streams. Do not infer identity, health, emotion, personality or ability from movement. Recording or sensing must be explicit, purpose-bound and participant-controlled.

## 5. Cooperative governance questions

A creative cooperative or community archive needs rules for who may contribute, view, download, remix or publish; who controls infrastructure and administrator credentials; how credit, revenue, expenses and shared equipment are handled; how disputes and rights claims are resolved; how participants are protected; how archives are backed up and stewarded when a founder leaves; and what happens when a member withdraws or a project dissolves.

Technical decentralisation does not answer these institutional questions. They belong in the human governance record, with specialist legal review where required.
