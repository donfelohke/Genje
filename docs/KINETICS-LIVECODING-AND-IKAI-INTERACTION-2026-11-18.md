# GENJE — Kinetics, Livecoding & Reflective Interaction

**Date:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** RESEARCH / CREATIVE SYSTEMS FRAMEWORK  
**Owner:** GENJE owns creative practice, movement and performance context. IKAI owns reflective-computing interaction. WARENA owns infrastructure.

## 1. Purpose

Connect GENJE's existing livecoding, movement-score and Laban-related research into a coherent kinetics research programme. This is not a claim that a motion-capture pipeline, choreography engine, performer dataset or real-time integration has been implemented.

**GENJE ZA WARENA** is the cultural and creative field: Genge-rooted sound, AfroTech, algorithmic music, livecoding, movement and choreohacking. It is not a product, checkout or commercial division.

## 2. Working model

Kinetics is treated here as the study and creative use of movement, timing, force, trajectory, rhythm and change. It is not interchangeable with choreography, dance notation, biomechanics or computer vision.

| Domain | What it contributes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Dance / choreography | Phrase, intention, relation, improvisation and authored performance | A universal movement grammar |
| Kinetics / movement dynamics | Timing, trajectory, acceleration, force and change | Inner emotion or identity |
| Laban Movement Analysis | Descriptive movement concepts and qualitative observation | A diagnosis or objective personality profile |
| Labanotation / Kinetography Laban | Detailed movement score and reconstruction conventions | A complete account of context or meaning |
| Motif notation | Selective representation of salient movement ideas | Full transcription |
| Livecoding | Human-authored, executable performance systems | Automatic artistic authorship |
| IKAI reflection | Participant-directed review of chosen records | Authoritative interpretation of a performer |

Keep the method appropriate to the question. A choreographic score, a kinetic measurement and a livecoded event log are different artefacts and should not be collapsed into one dataset.

## 4. Kinetics data model — participant-authored first

Start with notation and human annotation, not continuous sensing.

A movement record may contain:
- work/session identifier and date;
- performer-authored intent and context;
- score/notation reference and version;
- phrase or event boundaries;
- tempo and timing annotations where meaningful;
- spatial pathway or orientation described at the level required by the work;
- sound/code event links;
- performer corrections and alternative readings;
- consent, credit, rights and publication scope;
- uncertainty, omissions and interpretive notes.

Do not collect biometric or motion data merely because a device can. If later sensing is justified, record sensor type, sampling rate, calibration, coordinate frame, missingness, noise, consent, retention and deletion. Keep raw capture separate from interpretation.

## 5. Livecoding and movement interface

A possible performance system can expose synchronised but independently authored streams:

1. **Sound stream:** patterns, tempo, synthesis events and audio routing.
2. **Visual stream:** authored visuals and display timing.
3. **Movement stream:** score events, phrase markers and participant annotations.
4. **Reflection stream:** selected notes and post-performance review.

Use timestamps and explicit clock-domain mapping; do not assume audio, display and wearable clocks are synchronised. A shared event identifier can connect a sound cue to a score marker without claiming that one caused or explains the other.

The performer remains the author. The system can reveal timing or let the performer revisit a score; it must not label a gesture as anger, confidence, ability, health or intent.

## 6. Relationship to IKAI Mirror and Glass

- **IKAI Mirror:** a stationary reflective/display environment for participant-directed observation.
- **IKAI Glass:** voice and visual wearable interaction; capture remains explicit and bounded.
- **IKAI Space:** spatial interaction contracts shared by the mirror, wearable and workstation.
- **GENJE:** supplies authored movement, choreography, livecoding and performance context.
- **WARENA:** supplies reproducible local infrastructure and device/service integration.

GENJE does not own IKAI's sensor policy or participant memory. IKAI does not own GENJE's choreography, compositions or interpretation. WARENA does not convert either practice into a commercial product.

## 7. Research and evaluation questions

- Can a performer author, correct and reject every movement annotation?
- Does the system preserve improvisation and multiple readings?
- Can audio, visual and movement events be aligned without false precision?
- What is the minimum timing/spatial data required for a particular creative task?
- Can the workflow run offline with inspectable tools?
- Can the work be reconstructed from the score, source, runtime and performance notes?
- Are collaborators, performers and source communities properly credited and consented?

## 9. Rights, consent and cultural context

A notation, recording, sample, code fragment or performance may have distinct creators and rights holders. A public link is not a reuse licence. Record permissions for performers, collaborators, samples, video, audio, code and cultural material. Do not treat African dance or movement as a homogeneous dataset or extract practice from its social, historical and community context.
