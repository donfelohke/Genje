# GENJE Creative Practice and Provenance Standard

**Status:** Practice and documentation standard  
**Domain:** Genge, AfroTech, livecoding, sound, movement, choreography, choreohacking and embodied research.

## Purpose

Preserve human authorship, cultural context and the practical conditions needed to understand or revisit creative work. A recording, concept note, source file or generated artefact alone does not prove reproducibility or that all included material is cleared for publication.

## Record each work or experiment

- Title, stable identifier, author(s), contributors, roles and date.
- Intent, context, medium and concise description.
- Cultural/source references with accurate attribution and contextual notes.
- Status: `LIVE`, `PROTOTYPE`, `RESEARCH`, `CONCEPT`, `HISTORICAL`, `SUPERSEDED` or `UNKNOWN`.
- Source repository and exact commit/version where applicable.
- Software, hardware, instruments, runtime and dependencies actually used.
- Audio/video routing, timing/clock assumptions, external media, samples and licences where relevant.
- Movement score, notation, spatial context and performer adaptations where relevant.
- Performance, rehearsal or reproduction instructions.
- What was actually tested, observed, rehearsed or performed.
- Known differences, limitations and unresolved questions.
- Consent, credits, rights and publication permissions for collaborators and depicted people.

## Cultural and ethical practice

- Preserve context for borrowed terms, practices, recordings and references.
- Distinguish attested usage from interpretation, testimony, memory and creative intention.
- Do not imply endorsement by a community, institution or source without evidence.
- Distinguish original material from samples, tools, recordings and third-party assets.
- Respect collaborator consent, privacy, credit and licence conditions.
- Treat embodied practice as situated human work, not a generic dataset for judging people.
- Do not publish private rehearsal footage, messages or participant information without appropriate permission.

## Reproducibility

When a work is claimed to be reproducible, document exact source version, dependencies, environment, hardware, setup, routing and execution steps. Record test conditions and observed results. Use “reproduction attempted”, “partially reproducible” or “not yet verified” where more accurate. A recording proves that an occurrence was captured; it does not prove that the underlying work can be reproduced.

## Public presentation

Describe the actual work and its status in plain language. Keep research notes and source evidence available where appropriate, but do not make private or rights-restricted material public merely to improve the portfolio. Never inflate a proposal, tool list or repository document into a claim of a released platform or public performance.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Human authorship and contributors credited.
- [ ] Cultural/source context documented.
- [ ] Rights, permissions and consent checked.
- [ ] Status and reproducibility claims match evidence.
- [ ] Technical setup and dependencies recorded where relevant.
- [ ] Movement/participant privacy and accessibility considered.
- [ ] Private or sensitive material excluded.
- [ ] Changes reviewed through a branch and pull request.
