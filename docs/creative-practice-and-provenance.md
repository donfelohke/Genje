# GENJE Creative Practice and Provenance Standard

**Status:** Practice and documentation proposal  
**Domain:** Genge, AfroTech, livecoding, movement, choreohacking and embodied research.

## Purpose
Preserve human authorship, cultural context and the practical conditions needed to understand or revisit creative work. A recording, concept note or source file alone does not prove reproducibility or that all included material is cleared for publication.

## Record each work or experiment
- Title, author(s), contributors and date.
- Intent, context and short description.
- Cultural/source references with accurate attribution and contextual notes.
- Status: PROPOSED, RESEARCH, PROTOTYPE, LIVE, HISTORICAL, SUPERSEDED, ABANDONED or UNKNOWN.
- Source repository and exact commit/version where applicable.
- Software, hardware, instruments, runtime and dependencies.
- Audio/video routing, external media, samples and licences where relevant.
- Performance, rehearsal or reproduction instructions.
- What was actually tested, observed or performed.
- Known differences, limitations and unresolved questions.
- Consent, credits, rights and publication permissions for collaborators and depicted people.

## Cultural and ethical practice
- Preserve the context of borrowed terms, practices, recordings and references.
- Do not imply endorsement by a community, institution or source without evidence.
- Distinguish original material from samples, tools, recordings and third-party assets.
- Respect collaborator consent, privacy, credit and licence conditions.
- Treat embodied practice as situated human work, not a generic dataset for judging people.
- Do not publish private rehearsal footage, messages or participant information without appropriate permission.

## Reproducibility
When a work is claimed to be reproducible, document exact source version, dependencies, environment, hardware, setup and execution steps. Record test conditions and observed result. Use “reproduction attempted” or “partially reproducible” where more accurate.

## Public presentation
Describe the actual work and its status in plain language. Keep research notes and source evidence available where appropriate, but do not make private or rights-restricted material public merely to improve the portfolio.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Authorship and contributors credited.
- [ ] Cultural/source context documented.
- [ ] Rights, permissions and consent checked.
- [ ] Status and reproducibility claims match evidence.
- [ ] Technical setup and dependencies recorded where relevant.
- [ ] Private or sensitive material excluded.
- [ ] Changes reviewed through a branch and pull request.
